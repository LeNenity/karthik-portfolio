INTERNSHIP · 8 APRIL TO 26 AUGUST 2024

IoT Intern
at iWOW Connections.

I helped prepare connected devices, check utility-meter readings and support technicians during site work.

My attachment involved both hands-on equipment work and the records behind it: configuring modems, investigating readings, checking connectivity and documenting what needed attention.

600+4G modems and IoT sensors configured
300+Modbus RTU modems configured
1,200+Utility meters monitored
01

Device preparation

A large part of the work happened before a device reached its installation site. The configuration had to match the meter it would read.

  • Prepared 4G modem configurations for the correct meter IDs, installed SIM cards and recorded the device details.
  • Configured Modbus RTU modems for power and BTU meters, including devices used with Janitza power meters.
  • Prepared S47 pulse counters and Itron sensors, connected the wiring and checked the setup with a small water meter.
  • Helped onboard S47 devices to the LoRaWAN network server and checked that they were sending data before site work.

During the NUS work, I also helped reconfigure installed modems to read additional parameters such as power factor and current, rather than energy consumption alone.

02

Meter checks and troubleshooting

I checked water, electricity and BTU meter data, then recorded issues so my supervisor and the technicians could follow up.

  • Reviewed offline alerts and latest readings in Pandogrid and maintained the follow-up lists.
  • Checked water-meter calibration using the initial reading, pulse count and multiplier to help identify configuration errors.
  • Reviewed BTU readings for flow and temperature-difference issues, and compiled meters that needed troubleshooting.
  • Compared two BTU meters on a bench setup using temperature sensors and checked whether their readings changed together.
  • Helped set up replacement flow meters under my supervisor’s guidance and monitored the readings afterwards.

I also checked device status and battery records for other sensor deployments. This included distinguishing a tracker in standby mode from one that had stopped communicating.

03

Fieldwork and documentation

Site work helped me see how the devices, communications network and records fitted together.

  • Assisted with meter work in AHU rooms and electrical risers at NUS, working alongside my supervisor and technicians.
  • Compiled packet success rate (PSR) checks in Excel and made graphs to show how results compared with the project’s 70% target.
  • Helped check gateway status and update gateway location records and installation photographs.
  • Supported RSSI signal tests at Changi Airport Terminal 2, providing live reading updates and organising the results by sensor and time.
  • Joined monthly ESSO electricity-meter reading rounds, checked meter serial numbers and recorded photographs for billing follow-up.

The documentation mattered as much as the reading itself. A clear device ID, location, timestamp and remark made it easier for the next person to check the same issue.

04

What I learned

The attachment gave me practice working through a problem before escalating it. An unusual reading could come from a sensor, a calibration setting or a connection issue, so checking the records and the physical setup both mattered.

I became more careful about configuration details and documentation, and learned from the technicians while helping with preparation, testing and site visits.
